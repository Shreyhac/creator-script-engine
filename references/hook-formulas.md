# Hook Formulas — building a bank, not a list

The hook is the variable part. It is the only thing you write fifteen times, so
it needs structure or it degenerates into fifteen rewordings of one sentence.

---

## 1. Extract the formula from the reference

Take the reference overlays and blank out the parts that could change:

> "I could KISS the flight attendant who showed me this"
> "I could MARRY the graphic designer who showed me this site"

→ **`I could [VERB] the [PERSON] who showed me this [THING].`**

Three slots. Now you can enumerate instead of brainstorm. Write the formula down
explicitly in `TEMPLATE.md` — it is the most reusable thing in the whole document,
and it is what lets a teammate add hook #16 six weeks from now without asking you.

Note which slot carries the meaning. In the example above it is `[PERSON]` — it
names who you'd normally have to hire, and the twist is that nobody was hired.
Get that slot wrong and the hook stops pointing at the product.

---

## 2. Bank by ANGLE, not by wording

Fifteen synonyms is a bank of one. Group hooks so each group is a genuinely
different emotional route to the same payoff, then write 4–6 per group.

Angles that reliably work:

| Angle | Emotional job | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Over-the-top gratitude** | "this changed things for me" | I could MARRY / KISS / ADOPT whoever showed me this |
| **Insider secret / withholding** | "you weren't supposed to see this" | delete this before it goes viral, but… |
| **Disbelief** | "this can't be real" | no way this is free / I had to check twice |
| **Stakes / sacrifice** | "I'd give something up for this" | I'd sell a kidney / my whole paycheck |
| **Confession** | "I feel guilty about this" | I've been passing this off as my own work 🤫 |
| **Us vs them** | "someone is overcharging you" | my agency charged me $4k for what this does |

Rules of thumb: 3–4 angles × 4–5 hooks each = a healthy bank of 15–20. If one
angle is much easier to write than the others, that is a signal it is the right
lane — lead with it, but still ship the others so you can read the data.

---

## 3. Writing rules for burned-in text

These are cosmetic until you see them wrong on a phone screen, at which point
they're the whole ballgame:

- **Punchy and lowercase-ish**, with the load-bearing word in CAPS.
- **One emoji max.** Two reads as an ad.
- **No em dashes on screen** — they don't read at thumb speed.
- **Numbers as digits** — "4k", "3 mins", "$22".
- **Short enough to read in under 2 seconds.** If you have to shrink the type to
  fit, it is too long. Roughly 8–12 words.
- **Written final.** What you put in `[overlay: …]` is what gets burned. Do not
  write a description of the line; write the line.

---

## 4. Ordering the bank

Rank by expected performance, not by the order you wrote them:

1. **Closest to a proven reference.** The hook nearest the reference clip's own
   line is the safest first shoot and the cleanest read on whether the format
   transfers at all.
2. **Highest-variance swings** next — the ones that might pop or might die.
3. **Experiments last** — new angles, alt reveals, format stress-tests.

Flag the #1 explicitly in `SCRIPTS.md` (`⟵ send this one first`) so nobody has to
re-derive the decision.

---

## 5. What kills a hook

- **It explains the product.** The hook's only job is emotion; the reveal
  explains. A hook that says what the tool does has already spent the payoff.
- **It asks for something.** No CTA in the hook. Ever.
- **It is about the creator, not the discovery.** "I've been working so hard on…"
  turns a discovery format into a vlog.
- **It doesn't set up the reveal.** Read the hook, then watch the reveal. If the
  reveal doesn't feel like an answer to the hook, the pair is broken — this is
  the most common quiet failure.
- **It's clever.** Puns and wordplay slow the read. Blunt beats clever at 3
  seconds.

---

## 6. Refreshing the bank later

The bank decays as hooks get used. To extend it without a full re-run:

1. Keep the formula and the angle groups fixed.
2. Look at which *angle* performed, not which wording.
3. Write 4–5 new hooks inside the winning angle, plus one new angle as a probe.
4. Reuse the same fixed beat footage — that's the whole point of having filmed it
   once.

Rewriting the formula is a re-run of the full skill, not a refresh. If the
formula stops working, the format is dying, not the hooks.
